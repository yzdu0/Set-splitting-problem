1    Introduction
An edge of a hypergraph is split by a Boolean coloring if it contains both colors. In the parameterized
Set Splitting problem, the input is a hypergraph and a target k, and the question is whether
some coloring splits at least k edges. The parameter counts edges that must be split; it does not
bound their cardinality. We allow arbitrary edge sizes.
    Lokshtanov and Saurabh [1] give a polynomial-time decision kernel with at most k vertices
and 2k edges. Specialized algorithms of Nederlof, van Rooij, and van Dijk [2] achieve O∗ (1.7171k )
time with exponential space and O∗ (1.8213k ) time with polynomial space. Here O∗ suppresses
polynomial factors. These are useful established reference points for the present transfer result.
    The recent manuscript The Exponential Time Hypothesis is False states subexponential exact-
counting algorithms for each ﬁxed CNF width [3]. A direct encoding of whether every hyperedge
splits uses clauses whose width equals the edge size. Replacing large OR gates by bounded-fan-in
circuits can introduce one auxiliary variable per incidence. Even after the decision kernel, this can
produce Θ(k 2 ) variables, which does not by itself give a subexponential dependence on k from the
imported quantitative bound.

   We avoid this expansion by counting selected subsets of unsplit edges. A selector need only
imply monochromaticity. Monochromaticity is a conjunction of pairwise equalities, so each guarded
condition is a clause of width at most three without an incidence variable. A cardinality circuit
on the selectors uses only O(m) additional variables. Its counts are binomial moments of the
unsplit-edge distribution, which can be inverted in polynomial time.

Contributions. The main unconditional reduction, Theorem 3.3, uses O(n + m) variables re-
gardless of edge rank. Theorem 4.1 transfers the two documented general-density counting rates
to full split proﬁles. Corollary 4.2 combines the transfer with the decision kernel. Section 5 proves
extensions to pinned colors, optimal colorings, and families of 2-CNF predicates.

External dependence and status. We treat the quantitative counting statements in Section 2
as explicit algorithmic premises. This paper neither reproduces their proofs nor independently
certiﬁes their formalization. The stronger manuscript rate and the weaker rate documented in its
formalization scope note are kept separate. This is a research draft, not a claim of external review
or publication priority. The proposed contribution is the quantitative, variable-eﬃcient application
of established counting ideas to unbounded-rank split proﬁles.

5    Pinned colors and other predicate families
Proposition 5.1 (Pinned proﬁles and optimal colorings). Theorem 4.1 continues to hold when any
subset of vertices has speciﬁed colors and the proﬁle counts only extensions of these pins. Under the
same premise, an optimal coloring of the original hypergraph can be found within the time bound (7)
and polynomial space.

Proof. Add unit clauses on the pinned color variables to every selector formula. This adds at most
n clauses and no variables, and leaves the moment interpretation valid on the restricted set of
colorings. Conﬂicting pins give the identically zero proﬁle.
    First ﬁnd the largest exponent b with positive coeﬃcient in PH . Process vertices in order.
Tentatively pin the next vertex to zero and compute the restricted coeﬃcient at b. Keep zero if
that coeﬃcient is positive; otherwise pin it to one. The invariant that some optimum coloring
extends the retained pins guarantees success. At most n + 1 proﬁle computations are used, adding
a polynomial factor. This is a search result in n + m; it does not assume a target-k solution-lifting
property of the decision kernel.

    The selector construction is not speciﬁc to equality predicates. The following formulation iso-
lates its scope.

6    Relation to polynomial identities and earlier approaches
The transform BH (w) = QH (1 + w) is a standard binomial-moment identity. For nonempty hy-
peredges, White’s coboundary polynomial [5] specializes to QH . For a nonempty selected edge e,
let H/e delete that occurrence and identify its vertices, retaining every other edge label. White’s
Theorem 9, specialized to two primary colors and two colors in total, yields

                               QH (z) = QH−e (z) + (z − 1)QH/e (z),

and therefore PH (z) = zPH−e (z)+(1−z)PH/e (z). We credit these identities as prior algebraic struc-
ture. The present reduction evaluates the coeﬃcients of the shifted polynomial through bounded-
width guarded formulas rather than traversing the deletion–contraction tree.
    An alternative route processes every edge of size at least r by that recurrence. A root-to-leaf
path has at most m decisions and at most ⌊(n − 1)/(r − 1)⌋ contractions. Thus the number of
leaves is at most

At a leaf a direct circuit encoding uses O(n + rm) variables. With a choice √of r balancing tree
size against SAT cost, the sparse counting premise gives an exponent of order s log log s/(log s)1/4 .
The selector reduction avoids both this branching overhead and the incidence-variable expansion.
Even the more conservative Count2 selector bound is asymptotically smaller than that exponent,
since (log log s)3/2 /(log s)1/4 → 0.
    The imported SAT manuscript already notes a bounded-rank, all-edges splittability conse-
quence [3]. Our reduction handles unbounded rank, arbitrary targets, and exact proﬁles with
a variable bound depending only on n + m. This distinction is the relevant transfer issue.


7    Veriﬁcation, limitations, and open questions
The elementary construction has executable veriﬁcation harnesses, research/selector_moments.py
and research/verify_paper.py. They construct the guarded formulas, count them with a small
exhaustive DPLL oracle, and check the inverse transform against exhaustive assignments. Ver-
iﬁcation passed on 378 hypergraph proﬁles using 1,773 moment counts, 20 optimal-coloring
self-reductions, 80 general 2-CNF predicate proﬁles, and 20 CNF violation proﬁles. The manifest
also records 27 encoding-size checks. The accompanying code is a check of the reduction, not an
implementation of the imported fast counter. Appendix A speciﬁes the reproducibility interface.
Finite tests do not prove an asymptotic complexity claim; the size bounds, unique-extension
argument, inversion, and bit analysis above supply the proof of the reduction.
    The scope note and revised manuscript report diﬀerent quantitative counting rates. We have
not rechecked the entire external theorem or its Lean development. All corollaries are therefore
stated against the interfaces that they actually use. No practical speedup is established here; hidden
constants and polynomial degrees in an imported algorithm can dominate feasible instances.

   A fuller literature audit is needed before claiming priority for the application. The coboundary
polynomial, binomial inversion, cardinality circuits, and decision kernel are established ingredients.
The central algorithmic point is that one-way guarded predicates retain linear variable complexity
while permitting polynomially many clauses.
   Two quantitative questions remain. First, can unrestricted-rank split proﬁles be reduced to
ﬁxed-density 3-CNF using only O(n + m) variables? That would allow the sparse premise to
remove the remaining log log(n + m) factor. Second, can a sharper direct counting interface for
these guarded formulas improve the general-density rate? The present proof does not answer either
question.

A     Explicit reduction and output conventions
The following pseudocode describes the full-proﬁle algorithm. The counter is an interface argument;
supplying an exhaustive counter gives a small veriﬁcation implementation, while the complexity
theorem requires the stated subexponential interface.

      SplitProfile(H, Count3):
        n := number of declared vertices; m := number of indexed edges
        for t := 0 to m:
          declare x[1..n], y[1..m]
          for each nonempty edge e[i]:
            choose anchor a in e[i]
            for v in e[i] except a:
              add (not y[i] or not x[a] or x[v])
              add (not y[i] or x[a] or not x[v])
          add unique-extension exact-t circuit on y[1..m]
          F[t] := Count3(resulting formula, all declared variables)
        for j := 0 to m:
          a[j] := sum over t=j..m of (-1)ˆ(t-j)*binom(t,j)*F[t]
        return [a[m], a[m-1], ..., a[0]]
