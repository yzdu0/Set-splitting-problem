\begin{abstract}
We give a variable-efficient reduction from the complete split-count
distribution of an unbounded-rank hypergraph to exact counting for
3-CNF formulas. For a hypergraph with $n$ vertices and $m$ indexed edges,
the reduction makes $m+1$ counting calls, each with $O(n+m)$ variables
and $O(\sum_e |e|+m+1)$ clauses. Selectors mark arbitrary subsets of
monochromatic edges; fixing the number of selectors computes binomial
moments, from which the whole distribution is recovered exactly.
Combining this reduction with the general-density exact-counting theorem
stated in the October 2026 SAT manuscript gives deterministic
polynomial-space time
\[
  2^{O(k\log\log k/\sqrt{\log k})}+|I|^{O(1)}
\]
for \ssplit\ parameterized by the target number $k$ of split sets.
Using only the weaker rate documented in the accompanying formalization
scope note gives $2^{O(k(\log\log k)^2/\sqrt{\log k})}+|I|^{O(1)}$.
The same bounds, with $k$ replaced by $n+m$, compute the original
hypergraph's full distribution and an optimal coloring. The reduction
also applies to counting the number of satisfied predicates in a family
of 2-CNF formulas, and hence to the complete satisfaction profile of
arbitrary-width CNF. All subexponential bounds explicitly depend on the
imported counting theorem; the reductions and counting identities do not.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

An edge of a hypergraph is \emph{split} by a Boolean coloring if it contains
both colors. In the parameterized \ssplit\ problem, the input is a
hypergraph and a target $k$, and the question is whether some coloring
splits at least $k$ edges. The parameter counts edges that must be split;
it does not bound their cardinality. We allow arbitrary edge sizes.

Lokshtanov and Saurabh~\cite{LS09} give a polynomial-time decision kernel
with at most $k$ vertices and $2k$ edges. Specialized algorithms of
Nederlof, van Rooij, and van Dijk~\cite{NRD14} achieve $O^*(1.7171^k)$
time with exponential space and $O^*(1.8213^k)$ time with polynomial
space. Here $O^*$ suppresses polynomial factors. These are useful
established reference points for the present transfer result.

The recent manuscript \emph{The Exponential Time Hypothesis is False}
states subexponential exact-counting algorithms for each fixed CNF
width~\cite{SAT26}. A direct encoding of whether every hyperedge splits
uses clauses whose width equals the edge size. Replacing large OR gates
by bounded-fan-in circuits can introduce one auxiliary variable per
incidence. Even after the decision kernel, this can produce
$\Theta(k^2)$ variables, which does not by itself give a subexponential
dependence on $k$ from the imported quantitative bound.

We avoid this expansion by counting selected subsets of \emph{unsplit}
edges. A selector need only imply monochromaticity. Monochromaticity is
a conjunction of pairwise equalities, so each guarded condition is a
clause of width at most three without an incidence variable. A cardinality
circuit on the selectors uses only $O(m)$ additional variables. Its
counts are binomial moments of the unsplit-edge distribution, which can
be inverted in polynomial time.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
The main unconditional reduction, Theorem~\ref{thm:encoding}, uses
$O(n+m)$ variables regardless of edge rank. Theorem~\ref{thm:profile}
transfers the two documented general-density counting rates to full
split profiles. Corollary~\ref{cor:parameter} combines the transfer with
the decision kernel. Section~\ref{sec:extensions} proves extensions to
pinned colors, optimal colorings, and families of 2-CNF predicates.

\paragraph{External dependence and status.}
We treat the quantitative counting statements in Section~\ref{sec:premises}
as explicit algorithmic premises. This paper neither reproduces their
proofs nor independently certifies their formalization. The stronger
manuscript rate and the weaker rate documented in its formalization
scope note are kept separate. This is a research draft, not a claim of
external review or publication priority. The proposed contribution is
the quantitative, variable-efficient application of established counting
ideas to unbounded-rank split profiles.

\section{Preliminaries and counting premises}
\label{sec:premises}

A hypergraph is $H=(V,(e_i)_{i=1}^m)$, with $V=[n]$ and $e_i\subseteq V$.
Edges are indexed: parallel edges remain distinct occurrences. Put
\[
 s=n+m,\qquad J=\sum_{i=1}^m |e_i|.
\]
The universe and incidence lists are explicit. Let $L$ denote their bit
encoding length, including isolated vertices and edge labels; polynomial
factors may depend on $L+1$. Empty and singleton edges are never split.
For convenience, we call them monochromatic, including the vacuous
empty-edge case.

For $x\in\bits^n$, let $u_H(x)$ be the number of monochromatic edges and
$b_H(x)=m-u_H(x)$ the number of split edges. Define
\begin{align}
 a_j(H)&=|\{x\in\bits^n:u_H(x)=j\}|,\label{eq:aj}\\
 Q_H(z)&=\sum_{j=0}^m a_j(H)z^j, &
 P_H(z)&=\sum_{x\in\bits^n}z^{b_H(x)}
          =\sum_{j=0}^m a_j(H)z^{m-j}.\label{eq:polys}
\end{align}
Thus $P_H$ is the \emph{split-count polynomial}. For nonempty edges,
$Q_H$ is a specialization of the hypergraph coboundary polynomial studied
by White~\cite{White11}. Neither this polynomial nor its usual
deletion--contraction identity is claimed to be new.

All logarithmic expressions below are for sufficiently large arguments;
finitely many small cases are solved directly. For $\alpha\in\{1,2\}$,
write
\[
 f_\alpha(v)=\frac{v(\log\log v)^\alpha}{\sqrt{\log v}}.
\]
The choice of logarithm base changes only constants. A monotone extension
handles small arguments.

\begin{definition}[Counting interfaces]\label{def:count}
Interface $\Count_\alpha$ is a uniform deterministic algorithm that
exactly counts satisfying assignments of a 3-CNF with $v$ declared
variables and encoding length $\ell$ in time
\[
 (\ell+1)^{O(1)}2^{O(f_\alpha(v))}
\]
and space polynomial in $\ell+v+1$.
Interface $\Count_{\mathrm{sp}}$ has, for every fixed $D$, time
$\poly(\ell+v)2^{O_D(v/\sqrt{\log v})}$ on formulas with at most $Dv$
clauses, and polynomial space.
\end{definition}

The revised October 25 manuscript states $\Count_1$ and
$\Count_{\mathrm{sp}}$ as specializations of its fixed-width main
theorem~\cite{SAT26}. The accompanying scope note documents
$\Count_2$~\cite{Scope26}. We use width three throughout; no hidden
constant is allowed to depend on a growing edge size. The interfaces
count assignments to \emph{declared} variables, including isolated ones.
If an implementation drops such variables, their factor of two must be
restored. Unit and binary clauses can be padded by repeating literals
when an interface requires exactly three literal occurrences.

\section{Selector moments and a linear-variable encoding}
\label{sec:encoding}

Define the binomial moments
\begin{equation}
 F_t(H)=\sum_{x\in\bits^n}\binom{u_H(x)}{t},\qquad 0\le t\le m,
 \label{eq:moments}
\end{equation}
where $\binom{j}{t}=0$ for $t>j$. The selectors below count subsets of
monochromatic edges rather than requiring every monochromatic edge to
be selected.

\begin{lemma}[Binomial inversion]\label{lem:inversion}
The moments determine the whole profile by
\begin{equation}
 a_j(H)=\sum_{t=j}^m(-1)^{t-j}\binom{t}{j}F_t(H).
 \label{eq:inverse}
\end{equation}
Equivalently,
\begin{equation}
 P_H(z)=\sum_{t=0}^m F_t(H)z^{m-t}(1-z)^t.
 \label{eq:splitinverse}
\end{equation}
The transformation uses polynomially many bit operations in $n+m+1$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Let $B_H(w)=\sum_{t=0}^m F_t(H)w^t$. The binomial theorem gives
\[
 B_H(w)=\sum_x(1+w)^{u_H(x)}=Q_H(1+w).
\]
Hence $Q_H(z)=B_H(z-1)$; taking coefficients proves
\eqref{eq:inverse}. Substituting $z^{-1}$ and multiplying by $z^m$
gives the polynomial identity~\eqref{eq:splitinverse}.
Each $F_t\le 2^{n+m}$. Every term of~\eqref{eq:inverse} has magnitude
at most $2^{n+2m}$, and each partial sum has magnitude at most
$(m+1)2^{n+2m}$. Thus $O(n+m+\log(m+1))$ bits suffice, including
intermediate signed arithmetic. An $O(m^2)$-term implementation is
polynomial in the explicit output size.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Linear-size exact cardinality circuit]\label{lem:adder}
For $m$ Boolean inputs and $0\le t\le m$, the predicate that exactly $t$
inputs equal one has a fan-in-two Boolean circuit with $O(m+1)$ gates.
It admits an equivalent width-three Tseitin encoding with $O(m+1)$
auxiliary variables and clauses, and a unique auxiliary extension for
every satisfying input assignment.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Pad the input list with zeros to a power of two less than $2m$ when
$m>0$. Sum the bits in a balanced binary tree. At level $h$, there are
$O(m/2^h)$ additions on $O(h)$-bit operands, each requiring $O(h)$
bounded-fan-in gates. The total is
\[
 O\!\left(m\sum_{h\ge1}\frac{h}{2^h}\right)=O(m).
\]
Fixing the output bits to the binary expansion of $t$ uses $O(\log(m+1))$
unit conditions. Constants and $m=0$ require only constant overhead.

Introduce a variable for each gate and encode its full logical
equivalence. For example, $g\leftrightarrow(a\wedge b)$ is encoded by
\[
 (\neg g\vee a)\ \wedge\ (\neg g\vee b)\ \wedge\
 (g\vee\neg a\vee\neg b).
\]
OR, NOT, and a constant-size expansion of XOR have width-three encodings.
Once the input bits are fixed, each gate variable is uniquely determined
in topological order. Consequently satisfying inputs have exactly one
extension, while nonsatisfying inputs have none.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Selector encoding]\label{thm:encoding}
For every hypergraph $H$ and $0\le t\le m$, one can construct in
polynomial time a 3-CNF $\Psi_{H,t}$ with
\[
 v=O(n+m+1),\qquad c=O(J+m+1)
\]
variables and clauses, respectively, such that
$\#\Psi_{H,t}=F_t(H)$. The implicit constants are absolute and independent
of edge rank. Its bit encoding length is
$O((J+n+m+1)\log(n+m+2))$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Use the color variables $x_1,\ldots,x_n$ and introduce a selector $y_i$
for each indexed edge $e_i$. For a nonempty $e_i$, choose an anchor
$a_i\in e_i$. For every $v\in e_i\setminus\{a_i\}$ include the clauses
\begin{equation}
 (\neg y_i\vee\neg x_{a_i}\vee x_v),\qquad
 (\neg y_i\vee x_{a_i}\vee\neg x_v).
 \label{eq:guard}
\end{equation}
They express $y_i\Rightarrow(x_{a_i}=x_v)$. No extra variable is introduced
for an incidence. Empty and singleton edges need no guarded clauses,
because they are always unsplit. Finally add the exact-$t$ circuit and
the unique-extension encoding of Lemma~\ref{lem:adder} on the $m$
selectors.

Fix a coloring $x$. A selector may equal one only on a monochromatic
edge; on each such edge it may independently equal zero or one. The
exact-$t$ condition therefore permits exactly $\binom{u_H(x)}t$ selector
assignments. For every permitted selector assignment the gate variables
have one extension. Summing over $x$ proves the count identity.

The guarded clauses number
$2\sum_i\max\{|e_i|-1,0\}\le2J$.
The cardinality construction adds $O(m+1)$ variables and clauses.
The declared color variables contribute $n$, even if they are isolated.
All variable indices use $O(\log(n+m+2))$ bits, giving the asserted length.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[The one-way implication is essential]
The guards are deliberately \emph{not} equivalences between $y_i$ and
monochromaticity. Requiring equivalence would destroy the subset-counting
interpretation. In contrast, the auxiliary \emph{gate} constraints must
be equivalences to obtain a unique gate extension. These two roles are
different.
\end{remark}

\subsection{A small example}
Let $V=\{a,b,c\}$ and let the edges be $\{a,b\}$ and $\{b,c\}$. There
are two colorings with no monochromatic edge, four with one, and two
with two. Thus
\[
 (a_0,a_1,a_2)=(2,4,2),\qquad(F_0,F_1,F_2)=(8,8,2).
\]
Inversion returns $a_2=2$, $a_1=8-2\cdot2=4$, and
$a_0=8-8+2=2$. The resulting split-count polynomial is
$P_H(z)=2+4z+2z^2$. This example illustrates why counting only the
existence of a selector assignment would lose the needed information.

\section{Subexponential algorithms}
\label{sec:algorithms}

\begin{theorem}[Complete split profiles]\label{thm:profile}
Assume $\Count_\alpha$ for $\alpha\in\{1,2\}$. On every explicitly
represented hypergraph with $s=n+m$, all coefficients of $P_H$ can be
computed deterministically in polynomial space and time
\begin{equation}
 (L+1)^{O(1)}2^{O(f_\alpha(s))}.
 \label{eq:profiletime}
\end{equation}
There is no restriction on rank, parallel edges, or isolated vertices.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
For $t=0,1,\ldots,m$, construct $\Psi_{H,t}$ and call the one fixed
width-three counting algorithm. Theorem~\ref{thm:encoding} gives at most
$C(s+1)$ variables for an absolute $C$. The encoding lengths are polynomial
in $L+1$. Since $f_\alpha(C(s+1))=O(f_\alpha(s))$ for large $s$, each
call has the asserted exponential bound. The $m+1$ calls and the inversion
of Lemma~\ref{lem:inversion} add only polynomial overhead.

Make the calls sequentially, keeping only their integer outputs.
The formulas have polynomial length, each call uses polynomial space,
and all moment and transform values have polynomial bit length.
The output has $m+1$ coefficients of at most $n+1$ bits each. Thus the
whole algorithm uses polynomial space.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Parameterized decision]\label{cor:parameter}
Under $\Count_\alpha$, an instance $I$ of \ssplit\ with target $k$ can
be decided deterministically in polynomial space and time
\begin{equation}
 |I|^{O(1)}+2^{O(f_\alpha(k))}.
 \label{eq:paramtime}
\end{equation}
In particular, $\Count_1$ gives
$2^{O(k\log\log k/\sqrt{\log k})}+|I|^{O(1)}$, and $\Count_2$ gives
$2^{O(k(\log\log k)^2/\sqrt{\log k})}+|I|^{O(1)}$.
Both are $2^{o(k)}+|I|^{O(1)}$.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Trivial targets can be answered immediately. Apply the decision kernel
of Lokshtanov and Saurabh~\cite[Theorem~1]{LS09}. It either answers the
instance or returns an equivalent instance $(H',k_0)$ with at most $k$
vertices and $2k$ edges. Keep $k$ as the original parameter; no equality
between $k$ and $k_0$ is needed. Compute $P_{H'}$ by
Theorem~\ref{thm:profile} and answer yes exactly when a coefficient at an
index at least $k_0$ is positive.

The kernel has $s'\le3k$ and a representation of polynomial bit length
in $k+1$. Its polynomial factors are absorbed in $2^{O(f_\alpha(k))}$
for large $k$. The initial reduction costs polynomial time in $|I|$.
Since $(\log\log k)^\alpha/\sqrt{\log k}\to0$, the dependence is
subexponential in $k$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[A decision kernel is sufficient]
The computation in Corollary~\ref{cor:parameter} counts colorings of the
\emph{reduced} instance. We do not assert that the kernel preserves the
original coloring distribution. The full-profile theorem applies directly
to the original input with parameter $n+m$, whereas the target-$k$
corollary is a decision result.
\end{remark}

\begin{corollary}[Linear incidence]\label{cor:sparse}
Assume $\Count_{\mathrm{sp}}$. For every fixed $B$, hypergraphs with
$J\le B(n+m+1)$ admit full-profile computation in polynomial space and
time
\[
 (L+1)^{O(1)}2^{O_B(s/\sqrt{\log s})}.
\]
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
The selector formula has $v\ge n+m$ declared variables and
$O_B(n+m+1)$ clauses. Its density is bounded by a fixed constant.
Apply $\Count_{\mathrm{sp}}$ and repeat the proof of
Theorem~\ref{thm:profile}.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Why the sparse rate cannot be used for every kernel.}
The kernel bounds vertices and edges, not incidences. It permits
$J=\Theta(k^2)$, so the selector formula may have $\Theta(k)$ clauses
per variable. The general parameterized bound therefore uses
$\Count_1$ or $\Count_2$, not $\Count_{\mathrm{sp}}$. Introducing
incidence variables to make a circuit encoding sparse would sacrifice
the linear variable bound.

\section{Pinned colors and other predicate families}
\label{sec:extensions}

\begin{proposition}[Pinned profiles and optimal colorings]\label{prop:pins}
Theorem~\ref{thm:profile} continues to hold when any subset of vertices
has specified colors and the profile counts only extensions of these
pins. Under the same premise, an optimal coloring of the original
hypergraph can be found within the time bound~\eqref{eq:profiletime}
and polynomial space.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Add unit clauses on the pinned color variables to every selector formula.
This adds at most $n$ clauses and no variables, and leaves the moment
interpretation valid on the restricted set of colorings. Conflicting
pins give the identically zero profile.

First find the largest exponent $b$ with positive coefficient in $P_H$.
Process vertices in order. Tentatively pin the next vertex to zero and
compute the restricted coefficient at $b$. Keep zero if that coefficient
is positive; otherwise pin it to one. The invariant that some optimum
coloring extends the retained pins guarantees success. At most $n+1$
profile computations are used, adding a polynomial factor. This is a
search result in $n+m$; it does not assume a target-$k$ solution-lifting
property of the decision kernel.
\end{proof}

The selector construction is not specific to equality predicates.
The following formulation isolates its scope.

\begin{theorem}[Families of 2-CNF predicates]\label{thm:predicates}
Let $\Phi_1,\ldots,\Phi_m$ be explicitly represented 2-CNF formulas on
the same $n$ declared Boolean variables, with $C$ clauses in total.
For $x\in\bits^n$, let $q(x)=|\{i:\Phi_i(x)=1\}|$.
The entire distribution of $q(x)$ reduces to $m+1$ exact 3-CNF counting
calls, each with $O(n+m+1)$ variables and $O(C+m+1)$ clauses.
Consequently $\Count_\alpha$ gives time
$\poly(L+1)2^{O(f_\alpha(n+m))}$ and polynomial space. A fixed bound
$C=O(n+m)$ permits $\Count_{\mathrm{sp}}$ and its sharper rate.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
For every clause $D$ in $\Phi_i$, include the guarded clause
$\neg y_i\vee D$, whose width is at most three. A selected predicate
must be true; an unselected predicate imposes no condition. Add the
same exact-$t$ selector circuit as before. For fixed $x$, its count is
$\binom{q(x)}t$. Lemma~\ref{lem:inversion} recovers the distribution.
An empty conjunction is true and imposes no guards; an empty clause
produces the unit guard $\neg y_i$, correctly making that predicate
unselectable. The size and space arguments are unchanged.
\end{proof}

Monochromaticity of a nonempty edge is the 2-CNF predicate
\[
 \bigwedge_{v\in e\setminus\{a\}}
 \bigl((\neg x_a\vee x_v)\wedge(x_a\vee\neg x_v)\bigr),
\]
so Theorem~\ref{thm:encoding} is a direct specialization.

\begin{corollary}[Arbitrary-width CNF satisfaction profiles]\label{cor:maxsat}
Under $\Count_\alpha$, the number of assignments satisfying exactly
$j$ clauses, for every $j$, of a CNF with $n$ variables and $m$ clauses
of arbitrary width can be computed in
$\poly(L+1)2^{O(f_\alpha(n+m))}$ time and polynomial space.
An assignment maximizing the number of satisfied clauses can be found
within the same asymptotic bound.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
For an original clause $D_i$, its falsification predicate is the
conjunction of the negations of all its literals. This is a 1-CNF and
hence a 2-CNF predicate. Apply Theorem~\ref{thm:predicates} to the
falsification predicates and reverse the resulting distribution.
Pinning and self-reduction work as in Proposition~\ref{prop:pins}.
\end{proof}

\section{Relation to polynomial identities and earlier approaches}

The transform $B_H(w)=Q_H(1+w)$ is a standard binomial-moment identity.
For nonempty hyperedges, White's coboundary polynomial~\cite{White11}
specializes to $Q_H$. For a nonempty selected edge $e$, let $H/e$ delete
that occurrence and identify its vertices, retaining every other edge label.
White's Theorem~9, specialized to two primary colors and two colors in total,
yields
\[
 Q_H(z)=Q_{H-e}(z)+(z-1)Q_{H/e}(z),
\]
and therefore
$P_H(z)=zP_{H-e}(z)+(1-z)P_{H/e}(z)$.
We credit these identities as prior algebraic structure.
The present reduction evaluates the coefficients of the shifted polynomial
through bounded-width guarded formulas rather than traversing the
deletion--contraction tree.

An alternative route processes every edge of size at least $r$ by that
recurrence. A root-to-leaf path has at most $m$ decisions and at most
$\lfloor(n-1)/(r-1)\rfloor$ contractions. Thus the number of leaves is
at most
\[
 \sum_{j=0}^{\min\{m,\lfloor(n-1)/(r-1)\rfloor\}}\binom mj.
\]
At a leaf a direct circuit encoding uses $O(n+rm)$ variables. With a
choice of $r$ balancing tree size against SAT cost, the sparse counting
premise gives an exponent of order
$s\sqrt{\log\log s}/(\log s)^{1/4}$.
The selector reduction avoids both this branching overhead and the
incidence-variable expansion. Even the more conservative $\Count_2$
selector bound is asymptotically smaller than that exponent, since
$(\log\log s)^{3/2}/(\log s)^{1/4}\to0$.

The imported SAT manuscript already notes a bounded-rank, all-edges
splittability consequence~\cite{SAT26}. Our reduction handles unbounded
rank, arbitrary targets, and exact profiles with a variable bound depending
only on $n+m$. This distinction is the relevant transfer issue.

\section{Verification, limitations, and open questions}

The verification harnesses are \path{research/selector_moments.py} and
\path{research/verify_paper.py}. They construct the guarded formulas,
count them with a small exhaustive DPLL oracle, and check the inverse
transform against exhaustive assignments. Verification passed on 378
hypergraph profiles using 1,773 moment counts, 20 optimal-coloring
self-reductions, 80 general 2-CNF predicate profiles, and 20 CNF
violation profiles. The manifest also records 27 encoding-size checks.
The accompanying code is a check of the reduction, not an implementation
of the imported fast counter. Appendix~\ref{app:reproduce} specifies the
reproducibility interface.
Finite tests do not prove an asymptotic complexity claim; the size bounds,
unique-extension argument, inversion, and bit analysis above supply the
proof of the reduction.

The scope note and revised manuscript report different quantitative
counting rates. We have not rechecked the entire external theorem or its
Lean development. All corollaries are therefore stated against the
interfaces that they actually use. No practical speedup is established
here; hidden constants and polynomial degrees in an imported algorithm
can dominate feasible instances.

A fuller literature audit is needed before claiming priority for the
application. The coboundary polynomial, binomial inversion, cardinality
circuits, and decision kernel are established ingredients. The central
algorithmic point is that one-way guarded predicates retain linear
\emph{variable} complexity while permitting polynomially many clauses.

Two quantitative questions remain. First, can unrestricted-rank split
profiles be reduced to fixed-density 3-CNF using only $O(n+m)$ variables?
That would allow the sparse premise to remove the remaining
$\log\log(n+m)$ factor. Second, can a sharper direct counting interface
for these guarded formulas improve the general-density rate? The present
proof does not answer either question.

\section{Explicit reduction and output conventions}
\label{app:reproduce}

The following pseudocode describes the full-profile algorithm. The counter
is an interface argument; supplying an exhaustive counter gives a small
verification implementation, while the complexity theorem requires the
stated subexponential interface.

\begin{quote}\ttfamily\small
SplitProfile(H, Count3):\\
\hspace*{1em}n := number of declared vertices; m := number of indexed edges\\
\hspace*{1em}for t := 0 to m:\\
\hspace*{2em}declare x[1..n], y[1..m]\\
\hspace*{2em}for each nonempty edge e[i]:\\
\hspace*{3em}choose anchor a in e[i]\\
\hspace*{3em}for v in e[i] except a:\\
\hspace*{4em}add (not y[i] or not x[a] or x[v])\\
\hspace*{4em}add (not y[i] or x[a] or not x[v])\\
\hspace*{2em}add unique-extension exact-t circuit on y[1..m]\\
\hspace*{2em}F[t] := Count3(resulting formula, all declared variables)\\
\hspace*{1em}for j := 0 to m:\\
\hspace*{2em}a[j] := sum over t=j..m of (-1)\^{}(t-j)*binom(t,j)*F[t]\\
\hspace*{1em}return [a[m], a[m-1], ..., a[0]]
\end{quote}

The return value is indexed by the number of \emph{split} edges. All
edges retain their occurrence labels, including duplicates. For $n=0$,
the one empty coloring still contributes one. Isolated vertices remain
declared variables, so they contribute correctly to every count. Pinning
adds units to the color variables before each counting call.

For independent reproduction, run
\begin{quote}\ttfamily\small
python3 research/verify\_paper.py
\end{quote}
from the repository root. This invokes the selector harness and writes
\texttt{paper/verification.json}, including deterministic seeds, check
counts, encoding sizes, and source hashes. The harness imports the
constant-fan-in circuit and exhaustive counting utilities in
\texttt{research.py}. It checks
every simple hypergraph on at most three vertices, including empty and
singleton edges, and seeded additional instances with parallel edges and
isolated vertices. Its assertions verify the guarded count identity,
binomial inversion, clause width, and a linear bound on the number of
variables. Additional pinned-color and predicate-family checks test the
extensions proved in Section~\ref{sec:extensions}. These finite checks
are not premises of the mathematical proof.

\section{Source separation}

For clarity, the proof dependencies are:
\begin{itemize}
\item Theorems~\ref{thm:encoding} and~\ref{thm:predicates}, and
Lemma~\ref{lem:inversion}, are elementary reductions with full proofs here.
\item Theorem~\ref{thm:profile} and the profile/search corollaries use
exactly the selected counting interface from Definition~\ref{def:count}.
\item Corollary~\ref{cor:parameter} additionally uses the established
decision kernel~\cite{LS09}; it does not assume a counting kernel.
\item Corollary~\ref{cor:sparse} uses the separate fixed-density
interface. It is not substituted into the general kernel bound.
\end{itemize}
